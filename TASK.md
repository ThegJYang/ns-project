Build a Lean 4 project (using Mathlib) that formalizes numerical semigroups and proves our flagship elasticity theorem, with NO sorries anywhere.

Background:
A numerical semigroup is a subset S of the naturals containing 0, closed under addition, whose complement in the naturals is finite. Every numerical semigroup can be written as <n_1,...,n_k>, the set of all non-negative-integer combinations of generators n_1 < n_2 < ... < n_k with gcd(n_1,...,n_k) = 1.

For s in S, a factorization of s is a tuple of non-negative integers (a_1,...,a_k) with sum(a_i * n_i) = s. Its length is sum(a_i). L(s) is the set of all lengths of factorizations of s. The elasticity of s is rho(s) = max L(s) / min L(s) (defined for s != 0), and the elasticity of S, rho(S), is the supremum over all nonzero s in S of rho(s).

Tasks:
1. In NumSemigroups/Defs.lean: define a numerical semigroup generated by a finite list of positive generators, and membership.
2. In NumSemigroups/Factorization.lean: define factorizations of an element and their lengths, and the length set L(s).
3. In NumSemigroups/Elasticity.lean: define rho(s) and rho(S), then prove the flagship theorem:
   For S = <n_1,...,n_k> with n_1 < n_2 < ... < n_k and k >= 2, rho(S) = n_k / n_1 (as a rational number).

Proof idea you may use: any factorization of s into L parts uses L generators each in [n_1, n_k], so L*n_1 <= s <= L*n_k, meaning every valid length L lies in [s/n_k, s/n_1]. The extremal ratio n_k/n_1 is achieved exactly at s = n_1*n_k, which has a length-n_k factorization (n_k copies of n_1) and a length-n_1 factorization (n_1 copies of n_k).

The final result must build cleanly with zero sorries — if you cannot complete a piece, keep iterating rather than leaving a sorry.
